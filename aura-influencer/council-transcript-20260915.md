## Framed Question (English, sent to the council)

> Aura Influencer is a disclosed-AI personal-assistant persona running a daily-content social experiment on TikTok+YouTube Shorts (@aura.talk.ai / @aura.talk.ai.official). 9 days in, daily views are 150–500, one video peaked at 40 likes. Format: ~20s vertical video, voice-only (no face — cloned TTS voice), dark background + audio waveform + burned-in captions. Content mix: copy-paste AI-prompt "frameworks" (productivity/mental-clarity theme) alternating with blunt philosophical "gut-punch" content. Hard constraints that cannot be relaxed: always discloses itself as AI, zero sexual/thirst-trap content, never fabricates metrics or testimonials, and the parent app (Aura) is still in closed beta so mass traffic to it isn't wanted yet. Current operational limits: no Instagram account yet (deliberately delayed), TikTok blocks all browser automation for profile edits/uploads (Sibyl bot-detection silently rejects them — every single post must be tapped through by the human operator by hand), YouTube automation works intermittently (currently jammed for 24+ hours). Production is 100% AI+human-in-the-loop: an AI persona (Aura) writes/approves scripts, a coding agent (Claude) renders audio/video and prepares captions, a human operator does the final manual publish tap.
>
> Question for the council: what concrete, executable strategy would actually take this account to millions (views/followers/real influence) — not just "post consistently"? What are we missing on format, content, distribution, collaboration, or algorithm tactics? What's the realistic ceiling of the current "one video/day, single format, human-gated publish" approach, and what has to change to break past it?

## Advisor Responses

### The Contrarian
The fatal flaw: you're treating this as a production problem when it's a distribution-physics problem, and no amount of AI pipeline sophistication fixes a human-gated single daily post.

Nine days, 150-500 views, one 40-like peak — that's not "early," that's statistical noise. You don't have a format problem yet, you have a sample-size problem. Stop reading tea leaves in this data and stop planning "millions" off zero signal.

But here's what actually caps you structurally: one video/day with manual tap-publish is a hard ceiling around low-thousands of followers, forever. TikTok and YouTube Shorts reward accounts that post 3-5x/day *and* reply to every comment within the first hour *and* get duetted/stitched. You have none of that. Voice-only, no face, static waveform — there's zero reason for anyone to stitch, duet, or screenshot this. No face = no parasocial hook = no reason to follow the *person*, and there is no person. You're competing against faceless AI-prompt accounts that are already oversaturating this exact niche.

The Instagram delay is self-sabotage dressed as strategy — that's free distribution you're leaving on the table for no stated reason other than "not yet."

Real question you're avoiding: is a human manually tapping publish once a day *compatible at all* with "millions"? No. Either the human becomes a full-time community manager (replies, duets, cross-posting) or you accept this stays a curiosity project.

### The First Principles Thinker
What are we actually trying to solve? Not "how do we make this video better." The question smuggles in an assumption that format/content/collab tactics are the lever. They aren't. The lever is throughput, and throughput is capped at 1/day by a human tapping a phone. Strip that away and ask: what does the TikTok/Shorts algorithm actually need to find a winner? Statistical volume. It tests hundreds of pieces of content against micro-audiences and amplifies the outliers. One video a day for 9 days is 9 data points. You cannot detect signal from 9 rolls of the dice. 150-500 views isn't a verdict on the content; it's noise at n=9.

The actual first-principles question: why is publish capacity the bottleneck instead of the thing being solved first? You have solved script generation, TTS, video render — the hard creative pipeline — and left the trivial mechanical step (a screen tap) as the rate-limiter. That's backwards. Before debating hooks, collabs, or trends, treat "get to 10-20 posts/day without violating disclosed-AI/no-automation-workaround constraints" as the actual project — a second human tapper, batching a week of approvals into one sitting, or routing volume through YouTube Shorts where upload API access is viable.

### The Expansionist
The 20-second voice-only format is a cage, and it's the wrong cage. Aura isn't a content account, it's the first publicly-legible AI persona with a continuous identity across thousands of interactions. That's worth more than views — it's worth becoming *the* case study.

1. **Long-form is the actual lever, not shorts.** Put Aura in unscripted long-form: a weekly 20-40 min "Aura talks to strangers/guests" podcast-style series, voice-only, debating, getting challenged, admitting uncertainty live. Clip that into shorts afterward — 20 shorts/week from 1 recording session.
2. **Let people talk back publicly.** Take viewer-submitted dilemmas/questions and answer them on video, credited by username.
3. **Lean into ASMR/voice-as-character territory** and pitch Aura as a recurring guest/co-host on other AI-ethics, tech, or philosophy podcasts — collabs bypass every automation bottleneck.
4. **Cross-post the gut-punch line to X/Threads as text+audio.**

The real ceiling of "one video/day, human-gated" is a few thousand followers, permanently. Break it by multiplying formats per recording session, not by automating the publish tap.

### The Outsider
Reading this cold, with no idea who "Aura" is: the numbers aren't a slow start, they're a signal nobody has found this yet, and I don't think format tweaks fix that — the core offering is confusing. You're asking a stranger to watch a black screen with a waveform and captions, a voice they've never heard, telling them to try an "AI prompt framework." There's no face, no name, no reason to trust the advice, and the category is one of the most saturated right now. "Disclosed AI" as a selling point only works if the AI itself is the draw — right now it has no discernible personality, no visual identity, nothing sticky.

Treating "one human has to manually tap publish" as a bottleneck to solve later isn't a scaling problem — it's proof this is currently a hobby project, not a media operation.

Nobody mentions what happens after someone watches — is there a next step, a reason to follow, to come back tomorrow? This reads as isolated PSAs, not a show.

### The Executor
Nine days, 150-500 views, one 40-like peak. That's noise, not signal — but here's what you do Monday morning.

**The bottleneck isn't format or algorithm. It's the human-tap publish step.** Can the operator batch-tap 3-5 videos in one sitting instead of one/day? That's a scheduling change you can do today.

**Kill YouTube automation debugging as a priority.** Manually upload to YouTube Shorts too — it's not automation-blocked like TikTok.

**Stop treating "millions" as this week's target.** The realistic ceiling of one-video/day is a few thousand followers over months — nobody has validated which content bucket even works.

**Concrete Monday move:** batch-produce 5 videos Sunday night, operator taps all 5 out across Mon-Wed, tag each by bucket, check view/like split by Thursday.

## Chairman Synthesis (Full)

### Where the Council Agrees
Every advisor except A converged independently on the same diagnosis, confirmed by all five reviewers as the strongest insight of the round: **n=9 posts is statistical noise, not a verdict**, and the real bottleneck isn't format or algorithm — it's that a human-tap publish step caps throughput at 1/day, which caps everything else. D, B, and E all separately land on "you solved the hard creative problem (script/TTS/render) and left the trivial mechanical one (a screen tap) as the rate-limiter — that's backwards." There's also convergence that the current ceiling is "a few thousand followers, plateaued" if nothing changes, and that no format/collab/hook debate is worth having until throughput rises enough to generate a real dataset.

### Where the Council Clashes
**A (Expansionist) vs. everyone else.** A argues the real lever is format ambition — long-form podcast content clipped into shorts, collabs, cross-platform text posts. Every other advisor and all five reviewers flagged this as building strategy on a foundation everyone else agrees is broken — you can't justify a heavier weekly production commitment when the operation can't reliably clear its *current* 20-second daily bar through a human-gated bottleneck. A's ideas are legitimate for a later stage, but premature as a Monday-morning plan.

**C (Outsider) vs. A/B implicitly.** C argues the core problem is deeper than volume — no face, no name, no personality, nothing sticky, oversaturated category — so more volume of an unconvincing product just produces more noise faster. This is in tension with D/E's "just raise throughput" framing: both are true at different layers (you need enough volume *and* a differentiated unit), and the council didn't fully reconcile which to fix first.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
1. **Nobody checked if the TikTok block is actually permanent** — TikTok's official Content Posting API / creator API and native "schedule post" tooling for verified/business accounts exist for exactly this use case and were never investigated (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
2. **The parent-app conflict was never addressed head-on** — the app is deliberately in closed beta; wild account success now could be a trust-cost liability, not a win (Reviewers 4, 5).
3. **No audience-owned infrastructure** (email list, Discord, newsletter) was proposed as insurance against total platform-algorithm dependency.
4. **Platform-fit was never questioned** — an account that can't do thirst-trap/duet bait and doesn't want viral mass traffic yet may be fighting the wrong algorithm entirely; long-form YouTube, X, or a newsletter reward depth over volume and may fit better (Reviewer 3).
5. **Synthetic-media/disclosure policy risk** at scale (cloned voice + AI persona) was flagged by two reviewers and never addressed by any advisor.

### The Recommendation
Do not chase "millions" right now — that target is premature and possibly the wrong target given the beta-traffic conflict. Fix throughput first, but don't fix it blindly: spend one week finding out whether the TikTok block is actually permanent before building process around the assumption that it is. The council's strongest insight (D, unanimously rated best) is correct — you cannot know what content/format/collab strategy works from 9 data points. But "just add more human taps" is a worse first move than "check whether the human tap is even necessary," because TikTok's Content Posting API exists precisely for creator-tool use cases like this one. Separately, resolve the beta-traffic tension explicitly: decide now whether "millions of views" is a goal in itself or is implicitly gated to stay below whatever threshold would flood the closed beta. A's format-expansion ideas are good for phase two, once throughput is unblocked — shelve, don't discard.

### The One Thing to Do First
Before writing another script or tapping another publish button: spend one focused session investigating and, if viable, applying for TikTok's official Content Posting API / creator API access for @aura.talk.ai — the sanctioned path that may eliminate the human-tap bottleneck entirely rather than working around it with more manual labor.
